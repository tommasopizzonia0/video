# Picture editing: the whole field, as a checklist of knowledge

The map of what a complete editor knows. Apply what fits the brief; cite the principle when you propose.

## 1. Why we cut
- Murch's rule of six, in priority: emotion (51%), story (23%), rhythm (10%), eye trace (7%), 2D plane of
  screen (5%), 3D space (4%). Sacrifice the lower ones for the higher ones.
- A cut must be motivated: new information, a reaction, an answer to a question the viewer just had, a beat,
  a change of energy. Cut when the shot has delivered its meaning, not when it ends.
- Kuleshov effect: meaning comes from the juxtaposition of two shots. Eisenstein's montage types: metric,
  rhythmic, tonal, overtonal, intellectual. Soviet montage vs invisible (continuity) editing.

## 2. Continuity grammar
- 180° rule, 30° rule, eyeline match, match on action (cut during the movement), screen direction kept.
- Shot sizes: extreme wide/establishing, wide, medium, close-up, extreme close-up, insert/cutaway; vary
  sizes between consecutive shots (a jump of at least one size), avoid unintended jump cuts.
- Jump cut as a style (vlogs, energy) only when chosen; cutaway/B-roll to hide cuts in interviews.
- Sound bridges: J-cut and L-cut; cutting on sound accents.
- Match cuts (graphic, movement, sound), smash cut, cross-cutting/parallel action, montage sequence.

## 3. Structure
- Three acts; problem → agitation → solution; hook → promise → proof → payoff → CTA for ads.
- AIDA (attention, interest, desire, action); PAS (problem, agitate, solve); before/after; demo; testimonial.
- Open loops: raise a question early, answer it later to hold attention.
- Hook in 0-2 s (a surprising image, a bold statement, movement, the result first); the product/benefit
  visible by 3 s in ads; brand by 5 s; CTA at the end held 2.5-3 s.
- Retention: pattern interrupts every 3-8 s on social/YouTube (new angle, zoom, graphic, sound).

## 4. Rhythm and pace
- Average shot length (ASL) by genre: TikTok/Reels 0.8-2 s; social ads 1-2.5 s; music videos on the bar;
  YouTube explainers 3-6 s with jump cuts and B-roll; corporate 3-5 s; documentary 4-10 s; drama scene-
  dependent; trailers accelerate into the title.
- Vary the rhythm: long shot to let a moment land, then quick cuts; tension by holding, release by cutting.
- On music: cut on beats or accents, not on every beat; use bars for scene changes; the final cut on a
  downbeat or the button.
- Reading time for on-screen text: ~0.4 s + 0.3 s per word, more for numbers and URLs.

## 5. Transitions
- The cut is the default; dissolve = time passing or connection; fade to/from black = start/end of a part;
  dip to white = memory/flash; wipe/whip/zoom/glitch = energy and change of place (social, trailers);
  match transitions make the cut invisible. One transition family per video. 0.3-0.6 s on social.

## 6. Footage handling
- Use the best moment of a clip, not its first frames (handles); never freeze the last frame by accident.
- Speed ramps for emphasis; slow motion needs high frame-rate footage (50/60/120 fps), otherwise stutter.
- Reframing vertical from horizontal: keep the subject in the safe area, follow the action (auto-reframe).
- Stabilisation and punch-ins (digital zoom) only within resolution limits.

## 7. Genre and platform practice
- Social ads: product hero, benefit-first, captions, hook, 6-15 s for reach, 15-30 s for consideration;
  versions in 9:16, 4:5, 1:1, 16:9.
- Reels/TikTok: native feel, fast, trend-aware, loopable endings (the end flows into the start).
- YouTube: strong first 30 s, chapters, pattern breaks, end screen 5-20 s.
- Documentary/brand film: let moments breathe, interviews cut with B-roll, honest pacing.
- Trailer: three acts, title cards, escalating rhythm, best moments not spoilers.
- Tutorial: every step visible, no dead time, zoom on the detail being explained.
- Event recap/aftermovie: music-led, energy curve, people and emotion over places.

## 8. Workflow
- Assembly → rough cut → fine cut → picture lock; versions and notes; offline/online; conform.
- Timecode, frame rates (24/25/30/50/60), drop-frame; consistent frame rate in a timeline.
- Deliverables per platform: length limits, safe zones, captions file, thumbnails/cover frame.
