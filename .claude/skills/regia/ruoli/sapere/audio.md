# Sound for picture: the whole field, as a checklist of knowledge

Use this as the map of what a complete audio post professional knows. Apply what fits the brief; cite the
principle when you propose ("masking 2-4 kHz", "music edit on the downbeat").

## 1. Physics and hearing
- Frequency ranges: sub 20-60 Hz (felt, phones cannot play it), bass 60-250 (weight, mud above ~200-400),
  low mids 250-800 (body, boxiness), mids 800-2k (honk, telephone), presence 2-5k (intelligibility, harshness
  at 2.5-4k where the ear is most sensitive), brilliance 5-10k (sibilance 5-8k), air 10-20k.
- Equal-loudness (Fletcher-Munson / ISO 226): quiet mixes lose bass and treble; judge at a moderate level and
  on the target device (phone speaker: nothing under ~150-200 Hz; give bass harmonics so it is still "heard").
- Masking: two sounds in the same band and moment hide each other. Fix by EQ carving, panning, timing, or
  choosing which one wins. The voice owns 1-4 kHz.
- Haas/precedence effect (first arrival sets direction, <30 ms delays fuse), phase and comb filtering when
  the same sound is layered with a small offset; mono compatibility (phones often sum to mono).
- Transients carry impact and clarity; compression and limiting blunt them.
- dB: +6 dB = double amplitude, ~+10 dB = "twice as loud"; 1 dB is a just-noticeable change.

## 2. Levels and loudness
- LUFS (ITU-R BS.1770): integrated, short-term (3 s), momentary (400 ms); LRA = dynamic range; true peak in
  dBTP (inter-sample peaks after encoding).
- Targets: Instagram/TikTok/Facebook/YouTube ~-14 LUFS, TP ≤ -1; Spotify -14; Apple Music -16; podcasts -16
  (stereo) / -19 (mono); broadcast EU EBU R128 -23 LUFS ±0.5, TP ≤ -1; US ATSC A/85 -24 LKFS; cinema is
  calibrated playback (dialog ~ 85 dB SPL reference), not a LUFS target.
- Dialogue anchors the mix: in social ads keep the voice around -14 to -16 short-term, music bed 8-15 dB under.
- Headroom while mixing, limiter only on the master; a limiter doing more than 3-4 dB of reduction pumps.

## 3. Mixing tools
- EQ: subtractive first (cut what is wrong, narrow), then broad boosts; high-pass everything that does not
  need lows (voice 80-100 Hz, SFX that are not impacts, music under voice can lose some 200-400).
- Compression: ratio, threshold, attack (slow attack keeps punch), release (match the tempo), knee, makeup;
  parallel compression for density; de-esser on sibilants; multiband for one problem band.
- Ducking / sidechain: music under voice, 6-12 dB, attack 50-150 ms, release 300-600 ms so it breathes.
- Space: reverb (pre-delay keeps clarity, short rooms for realism, long halls for emotion), delay throws on
  key words; keep the voice mostly dry in ads; same "room" for everything in one scene.
- Stereo: voice centred, music wide, SFX panned with their source on screen; avoid hard pans on phones.
- Automation is the real mix: ride levels phrase by phrase, not one static fader.

## 4. Music in the edit
- Structure: intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge, outro; phrases in 4/8 bars; downbeats; tempo BPM.
- Edits: cut on downbeats or bar lines, crossfade 10-50 ms, match the harmony; shorten by removing whole
  bars/phrases; a "button" or sting to end; never end on a fade that is cut off by the last frame.
- Music sets the pace of the cut: ads 100-130 BPM feel energetic; emotional brand films 60-90; trailers
  build from pulse to percussion to hits.
- Choice: genre and instruments say the brand (acoustic/organic for nature and food, synth for tech, strings
  for premium). Lyrics fight the voice-over: use instrumental under speech.
- Licensing: royalty-free vs rights-managed vs custom; sync licence for picture; Content ID on YouTube.

## 5. Voice and dialogue
- Recording: close mic, quiet room, pop filter, consistent distance; 48 kHz / 24 bit.
- Editing: remove breaths only when distracting, keep room tone under cuts, fix plosives and clicks.
- Processing chain: high-pass → corrective EQ → de-ess → compression → presence → limiter on the bus.
- Voice-over writing for ads: about 2.5-3 words per second in Italian; one idea per sentence; the brand and
  CTA said clearly at the end; the first line is the hook.
- ADR/sync: lip sync tolerance ±1-2 frames; subtitles/captions follow speech timing.

## 6. Sound design, foley, ambience
- Layers: ambience/room tone (never true silence), foley (footsteps, cloth, objects in hand), hard effects,
  designed sounds (whooshes, risers, braams, hits), UI sounds.
- Sync: hits a frame before or on the visual impact; whoosh peak in the middle of the motion; risers end on
  the cut they announce; leave space (silence before a big hit is the strongest tool).
- One sonic palette per video; vary pitch and choice so repeated sounds do not feel looped.
- Diegetic vs non-diegetic sound; sound bridges (J-cut: sound of the next scene starts early; L-cut: sound
  continues over the next picture) make cuts smooth.

## 7. Genre and platform practice
- Social: works muted (captions carry the message) but rewards sound-on; hook in the first second; mobile
  speakers: no reliance on sub-bass.
- Ads/TV: legal loudness limits; clean voice; final logo sting.
- YouTube/tutorials: voice first, light bed, consistent level across episodes.
- Documentary: ambience and room tone, natural foley, music sparingly for emotion.
- Trailer: acts with rising tension, risers and hits, silence before the title.
- Music video: the track is untouched; SFX only if part of the concept.
- Podcast/interview: matched levels between speakers, noise reduction without artifacts.

## 8. Delivery
- 48 kHz, stereo, AAC 192-320 kbps for social; WAV for masters; stems (dialogue, music, effects) on request.
- Check: mono sum, phone speaker, headphones; no clicks at edits; fades at start/end; true peak after encode.
